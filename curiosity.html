Personal Philosophy

Here's how I currently understand the world. It may change.

Curiosity as a Moral Trait

Curiosity, to me, isn't just an intellectual habit. It's an ethical one.

+

Curiosity is how I show respect for the world and the people in it. To be curious is to believe there's more beneath the surface. There's always more context, more nuance, more worth understanding before drawing conclusions.

When I'm curious, I'm choosing not to simplify something just to make it easier to hold. I'm choosing to stay open, to ask again, to listen longer than what's comfortable. In that sense, curiosity becomes a form of care. It's how I resist certainty that arrives too quickly.

I think a lot of harm comes from a lack of curiosity. When we assume instead of ask, we start deciding instead of listening. Curiosity slows that process down. It creates space for complexity. It reminds me that understanding is something you earn, not something you claim.

And this applies to people, too. You can only be curious about someone if you care about them. No agenda. No performance. Nothing except for humility and the willingness to look a little closer and let them be more than your first impression.

Learning as Devotion

I don't experience learning as accumulation. I experience it as devotion.

+

Learning, for me, isn’t about gathering knowledge or proving competence. It’s about returning, again and again, to something with attention and humility. It’s about staying long enough to let understanding deepen rather than rushing toward closure.

I think of learning as a practice that's almost reverent in nature. A way of saying that this matters enough for me to stay with it. It requires patience, openness, and a willingness to be changed by what you learn.

The things that have shaped me most weren’t things I mastered quickly. They were things I sat with, struggled with, and slowly grew into. Learning, to me, is less about knowing more and more about something and more about caring deeply enough to keep asking.

Questions as Care

I question things only when I care deeply about them.

+

I don't ask questions to challenge for the sake of it. I ask because something matters to me. Because I want to understand it well enough to treat it with respect.

Questioning is how I stay present. It's how I make sense of people, ideas, and systems that feel complex or unfinished. When I question something, I'm saying: this deserves more than a surface answer.

Some people see questions as doubt. I see them as attention. As a form of engagement. As proof that I'm invested enough to want clarity instead of comfort.

Growth and Change

I believe growth and change are synonyms

+

I don't think growth is linear, or even obvious most of the time. A lot of it looks like discomfort. Like realizing you might have been wrong about something. Like outgrowing a version of yourself without knowing what comes next.

To me, growth is noticing when something inside you has shifted and choosing to pay attention. It's letting yourself change without needing to justify it or package it nicely.

I've learned that growth isn't about becoming more impressive. It's about becoming more aligned. More honest with yourself. More willing to let go of what no longer fits, even when you don't yet know what will replace it.

On Meaning

I don't believe meaning is something we discover fully formed.

+

I think meaning is something we create, slowly, through attention and intention. It emerges from the patterns we notice, the questions we return to, and the care we extend toward the things that matter to us.

Meaning doesn't arrive all at once. It accumulates through small decisions, repeated values, and moments of quiet clarity. It lives in the space between curiosity and understanding, between intention and action.

To me, meaning isn't a destination. It's a process of noticing what resonates, and choosing to engage with it deeply.

On Attention

What we give our attention to shapes who we become.

+

Attention is one of the most powerful things we have. It shapes what grows in our lives, what we notice, and what we allow to influence us.

I try to be careful with mine. Intention matters, but attention is what actually determines the quality of your life. What you pay attention to slowly becomes what you care about.

Where attention goes, identity follows. So I try to place mine where I want to grow, and value the time around people, ideas, and spaces that challenge me in the right ways and help me become better, not just busier.

On Becoming

I don't think identity is fixed.

+

I think identity is something we practice. Something we revise. Something that shifts as we learn more about ourselves and the world around us.

I'm not interested in being a finished version of myself. I'm more interested in becoming — slowly, honestly, and with intention. I want to stay open to growth, to contradiction, to change. I want to be willing to learn when I was wrong, and understand why.

Who I am is shaped by what I choose to keep, what I choose to unlearn, and what I choose to move toward. Over time, those choices add up. They become direction. They become character.

On Destiny

I don't believe we're born with a prewritten path.

+

I don't think destiny is something waiting for us. I think it's something we build, quietly, through our choices.

Our lives are shaped by patterns. All of the habits we return to, the beliefs we carry, the effort we put in when no one is watching – it all ties back eventually. Destiny is the accumulation of those choices over time.

It's not fate. It's agency.

The way we think shapes how we act. The way we act shapes how we live. The way we live becomes the story we tell about ourselves.

That idea comforts me. It means small choices matter. It means growth is possible. It means becoming is always in motion.

On Forgiveness

Forgiveness has nothing to do with anyone but yourself.

+

I don't think forgiveness is about excusing harm or pretending something didn't happen. And it's definitely not about forgetting. To me, it's about releasing yourself from carrying something that no longer serves you.

Forgiveness is closely tied to acceptance. Not acceptance of what should have happened, but acceptance of what did. It's acknowledging reality as it is, without needing to relive it or rewrite it. Without asking it to be different before you allow yourself peace.

For me, forgiveness is an act of self-respect. It's choosing not to let resentment take up space in your inner world. It's deciding that your energy, your clarity, and your peace matter more than holding onto what can't be changed.

Sometimes forgiveness looks like reconciliation. Sometimes it looks like distance. Sometimes it's just a quiet decision you make for yourself, over time.

Either way, it isn't about them. It's about freeing yourself to move forward without carrying what was never yours to hold.
