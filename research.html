Research I've Loved

Academic curiosity, applied

Project Title

What I studied: How people make decisions when they're stressed.

Why I cared: Because understanding human behavior under pressure helps us build better systems and support each other better.

What I actually did: Ran experiments, analyzed data, talked to real people about their experiences.

What it taught me: Stress changes everything, and small design choices can make big differences in how people feel and act.

What I studied: Cognitive load theory and decision-making heuristics under acute stress conditions.

Why I cared: The intersection of behavioral psychology and system design reveals critical failure modes in human-computer interaction.

What I actually did: Conducted mixed-methods research combining controlled laboratory experiments (n=120) with semi-structured interviews, employing both quantitative analysis (ANOVA, regression models) and thematic analysis.

What it taught me: Stress-induced cognitive narrowing significantly impacts decision quality, and interface design can mitigate or exacerbate these effects through information architecture and feedback mechanisms.

Another Project

What I studied: How computers can help us understand language better.

Why I cared: Language is how we connect, and I wanted to see if machines could help us understand each other more deeply.

What I actually did: Built models, trained them on lots of text, tested how well they understood context and emotion.

What it taught me: Machines can find patterns we miss, but they still don't understand the human experience behind words.

What I studied: Natural language processing and computational linguistics, specifically contextual embeddings and sentiment analysis.

Why I cared: Advancing NLP capabilities has implications for human-computer interaction, accessibility, and cross-cultural communication systems.

What I actually did: Developed transformer-based models (BERT variants) fine-tuned on domain-specific corpora, evaluated using F1 scores, precision-recall curves, and human evaluation metrics.

What it taught me: While transformer architectures capture syntactic and semantic patterns effectively, they lack genuine comprehension of pragmatic meaning and cultural context, revealing fundamental limitations in current approaches.
