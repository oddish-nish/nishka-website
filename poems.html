What Confidence Was

I used to think I knew myself.

Sharp laugh. Sure steps.

A face that fit the story I told.

Maybe that was all it was,

comfort, not courage.


I called it confidence

but maybe it was just ease.

A mask that matched

what the world liked to see,

what I liked to see.


Now the surface has shifted

and with it, something in me.

Not broken,

but uncertain.

Unfamiliar in my own skin.


If love is only true

when it stays through the silence,

through the not-knowing,

through the coming undone,

then what is confidence?


Can it still be real

if it shakes?


Maybe it isn't a fortress.

Maybe it's a tide

that rises and falls,

gritty with sand and sting.


Maybe it's not in the mirror

but in how I return to it

with gentler eyes.


Maybe confidence

is not knowing who I am

but choosing to stay

and find out.

Stay (Revisited)

I've had many different meetings with love.


When I first met him, he lived next door.

He was always nice to me,

even in front of his cool guy friends.

I remember playing in his backyard,

a pretend family

we didn't know we were auditioning for.


It didn't take long to find love again.

He had a messy bowl cut

and perfect white teeth.

He could answer every question in class,

and he liked Helen, not me.

(It was the first time I learned

that love doesn't bend just because you want it.)


I didn't meet love for a while after that,

but love wrote to me anyway.

This time love wanted me,

but I wanted to be desired, not needed,

and so love felt like a weight I hadn't grown into.


Then love arrived overnight—

late conversations, wild dreams,

stories with no logic and no end.

But love becomes friendship

when life refuses to make space for it,

and we let it fade gently.


I once confused obsession for love,

mistaking possession for passion.

He haunted me,

and I shrank inside the outlines he drew for me.

That was not love.

That was erasure.


Love then visited in the form of a friend,

a gentle soul with bright hands

and a heart that stayed open even when I didn't.

He wanted a future I was too scared to inhabit,

and I was already unraveling in silence.


I stumbled into chaos

when I should have walked away clean,

too overwhelmed to see the edges,

too unsupported to hold myself steady.


And in that storm,

I hurt something kind

without ever meaning to.


And now love is quieter.

Less of a person,

more of a horizon.

Far away, yes,

but steady,

a small ember refusing to die out.


I see glimpses of it

in good conversations,

in peaceful mornings,

in the way I grow softer toward myself.


Every meeting with love

has arrived at the right time,

with the right person,

for the right duration,

teaching me what I could hold,

what I had to release,

and what I am finally ready for.


I don't ask love to stay anymore.

I ask myself to.

And one day,

when love comes again,

I will.

There's a Lot of Intimacy in Never Speaking Again

There is a lot of intimacy

in never speaking again.


Two strangers who remember each other

too precisely to be strangers.

Two histories that refuse to unfold

but never quite close.

Two paths that diverged at last,

now completely hidden by the shrubbery.

Two people left with outlines of each other

without ever touching their future.

Two dreams with bright new beginnings

still carrying the shadow of lessons before.

Two chapters closed in different books,

two versions of ourselves already outdated.


there is a lot of intimacy

in never speaking again,

in the shared discomforts

of not sharing anything,

in the quiet that settles

between one life and the next.


A thin thread of understanding

runs through that silence,

one neither of us will pull on

but neither of us will forget.

A Different Lifetime

in another version of the world,

we meet without the static.

he listens,

i stay.

no one flinches at honesty.


we still find each other,

but he doesn’t pull away at closeness

or joke his way out of honesty.

i don’t have to pretend

i’m not waiting for more.


we still laugh too loud,

but it lands softer.

he knows how to keep a promise,

and i don’t have to teach him

how to treat a heart gently.


and the love that was real

isn’t turned over for flaws,

its light measured and doubted,

its edges studied for proof it’s false.

no one calls a diamond an imposter

for being too bright to touch.


but that’s not this story.

this one ends in silence,

in the space where he could have reached,

and didn’t.


and i keep walking —

not heartbroken,

just aware

that some people only fit you

in lifetimes kinder than this one.

Warriors Wrestling Team

Today,

I mourned something

I thought I'd already given away.


Legacy isn't what you build.

It's what survives being left behind.


This is why mountains have a peak.

Nothing that rises

gets to keep rising forever.


I helped carry this place uphill.

Bruised. Sweat-soaked. Bloodied.

Belief heavier than my body.

Dreams stitched into the air by repetition.


At the top, we didn't celebrate.

We just stood taller.

Like excellence was the ground we walked on.


I handed the climb to someone else

and trusted gravity wouldn't notice.


I forgot

that descent is patient.

That erosion is quiet.


I came back years later.

The mountain was still here,

but the trail was gone.


This is why mountains have a peak.

Not because they fail,

but because they give everything

to get that high.


Coming down

is the price of having ever been there.

Resurrection

i mourned our past.

wrote an elegy for someone still alive.

set fire to the rain

and watched our memories hiss and curl into smoke

i survived.


i grew skin over the wounds.

i taught my ribs to close around the silence.

and then-

you re-entered my life.

soft-footed.

like i hadn't built a shrine

to what we used to be.

like i hadn't forgotten the way to it.

like the ivy that grows on walls- you came back.


you apologized.

and meant it.

and still, i looked at you

now a ghost that forgot hot to haunt.


should i believe in zombies?

in resurrections?

in connection that rises

after rigor mortis?

because you still laugh the same.

you still say my name like it matters.


but i don't know if i can

unburn what i buried

i don't know if i can ignore

the knot.

in a repaired red string.

i don't know if love

gets a second life

without limping.


so tell me, honestly:

which version of us is this?

and will it die again?
